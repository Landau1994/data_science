# 习题 P091：看守报告机制与三囚犯交换命运

## 题目

### 英文原题

> [!question] 英文原题
> **1.37** Here we will look at some variations of Example 1.3.4.
>
> (a) In the warden’s calculation of Example 1.3.4 it was assumed that if A were to be pardoned, then with equal probability the warden would tell A that either B or C would die. However, this need not be the case. The warden can assign probabilities $\gamma$ and $1-\gamma$ to these events, as shown here:
>
> | Prisoner pardoned | Warden tells A | |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | A | B dies | with probability $\gamma$ |
> | A | C dies | with probability $1-\gamma$ |
> | B | C dies | |
> | C | B dies | |
>
> Calculate $P(A\mid\mathcal W)$ as a function of $\gamma$. For what values of $\gamma$ is $P(A\mid\mathcal W)$ less than, equal to, or greater than $\frac13$?
>
> (b) Suppose again that $\gamma=\frac12$, as in the example. After the warden tells A that B will die, A thinks for a while and realizes that his original calculation was false. However, A then gets a bright idea. A asks the warden if he can swap fates with C. The warden, thinking that no information has been passed, agrees to this. Prove that A’s reasoning is now correct and that his probability of survival has jumped to $\frac23$!
>
> A similar, but somewhat more complicated, problem, the “Monty Hall problem” is discussed by Selvin (1975). The problem in this guise gained a fair amount of notariaty when it appeared in a Sunday magazine (vos Savant 1990) along with a correct answer but with questionable explanation. The ensuing debate even was reported on the front page of the Sunday New York Times (Tierney 1991). A complete and somewhat amusing treatment is given by Morgan et al. (1991) (see also the response by vos Savant 1991). Chun (1999) pretty much exhausts the problem with a very thorough analysis.

### 中文转述

> [!question] 中文转述
> **原题1.37。** 考察例1.3.4的若干变式。
>
> (a) 原例假定：若A获赦免，看守告诉A“B将被处死”或“C将被处死”的概率相等。现在改为以下机制：
>
> | 获赦免者 | 看守告诉A | 条件概率 |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | A | B将被处死 | $\gamma$ |
> | A | C将被处死 | $1-\gamma$ |
> | B | C将被处死 | 1 |
> | C | B将被处死 | 1 |
>
> 计算$P(A\mid\mathcal W)$随$\gamma$的函数，判断何时小于、等于或大于$1/3$。
>
> (b) 再令$\gamma=1/2$。看守告诉A，B将被处死后，A意识到自己原来的计算错误，便询问可否与C交换命运。看守认为没有传递任何信息而同意。证明此时A的推理正确，其存活概率跃升为$2/3$。
>
> 原文讨论：Selvin（1975）讨论了类似而略复杂的“Monty Hall问题”。这一形式曾刊于星期日杂志（vos Savant 1990），答案正确但解释有疑问，并引起广泛关注；争论甚至登上星期日《纽约时报》头版（Tierney 1991）。Morgan等（1991）作了完整且有趣的讨论，另见vos Savant（1991）的回应；Chun（1999）给出非常详尽的分析。

### 原例条件补充

例1.3.4有三名死刑犯A、B、C，州长等概率赦免一名。看守知道结果，只能向A指出B或C中将被处死的一人；若A获赦免，原例按等概率选择报告对象。$\mathcal W$专指报告“B将被处死”的事件。本段是已核对原印刷18／PDF49后的中文背景，不属于英文原题。

## 提示

> [!hint]- 提示
> 观察到的是“看守按特定机制报告B将死”，不是简单的“B没获赦免”。先写报告在A、B、C各自获赦免条件下的概率。

## 解答

> [!success]- 解答
> **证明范围：独立推导。** 沿用原例的均匀赦免模型：$A,B,C$分别表示相应囚犯获赦免，三者互斥、穷尽且各有概率$1/3$。$\mathcal W$表示看守说“B将被处死”，$0\le\gamma\le1$。
>
> **(a)** 报告机制给出
> $$
> P(\mathcal W\mid A)=\gamma,\quad P(\mathcal W\mid B)=0,\quad P(\mathcal W\mid C)=1.
> $$
> 因此
> $$
> P(\mathcal W)=\frac{1+\gamma}{3}>0,\qquad
> P(A\mid\mathcal W)=\frac{\gamma/3}{(1+\gamma)/3}=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}.
> $$
> 分母正，所以与$1/3$比较等价于比较$3\gamma$和$1+\gamma$：当$0\le\gamma<1/2$时小于，$\gamma=1/2$时相等，$1/2<\gamma\le1$时大于。同时$P(C\mid\mathcal W)=1/(1+\gamma)$，$P(B\mid\mathcal W)=0$。
>
> **(b)** 令$\gamma=1/2$，观察报告后仍有$P(A\mid\mathcal W)=1/3$，而$P(C\mid\mathcal W)=2/3$。看守同意按原题交换命运，则交换后A生存恰对应交换前C获赦免。因此新生存概率为$2/3$。
>
> 这不表示原来的赦免重新随机抽取，交换只是把A与C的原命运对调。若看守是否同意还依赖于未公开的真实赦免结果，需把同意事件也放入条件；原题采用看守同意此次交换的规定，未另给此类筛选机制。

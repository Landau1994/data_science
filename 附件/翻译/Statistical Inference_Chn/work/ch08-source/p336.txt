     If T(X) is a sufficient statistic for θ with pdf or pmf g(t|θ), then we might consider constructing
an LRT based on T and its likelihood function L∗(θ|t) = g(t|θ), rather than on the sample X and its
likelihood function L(θ|x). Let λ ∗(t) denote the likelihood ratio test statistic based on T. Given theintuitive notion that all the information about θ in x is contained in T(x), the test based on T should
be as good as the test based on the complete sample X. In fact the tests are equivalent.
Theorem 8.2.4    If T(X) is a sufficient statistic for θ and λ ∗(t) and λ(x) are the LRT statistics
based on T and X, respectively, then λ ∗(T(x)) = λ(x) for every x in the sample space.

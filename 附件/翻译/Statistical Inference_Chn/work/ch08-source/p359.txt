8.13 Let X1,X2 be iid uniform(θ,θ + 1). For testing H0 : θ = 0 versus H1 : θ > 0, we have two competing
       tests:

                                 φ1(X1) :     Reject H0 if X1 > .95,
                              φ2(X1,X2) :     Reject H0 if X1 +X2 > C.

       (a) Find the value of C so that φ2 has the same size as φ1.
      (b) Calculate the power function of each test. Draw a well-labeled graph of each power function.
       (c) Prove or disprove:  φ2 is a more powerful test than φ1.
      (d) Show how to get a test that has the same size, but is more powerful than φ2.

8.16 One very striking abuse of α levels is to choose them after seeing the data, and to choose them in such
      a way as to force rejection (or acceptance) of a null hypothesis. To see what the true Type I and Type II
      Error probabilities of such a procedure are, calculate size and power of the following two trivial tests:
       (a) Always reject H0, no matter what data are obtained (equivalent to the practice of choosing the α
           level to force rejection of H0).
      (b) Always accept H0, no matter what data are obtained (equivalent to the practice of choosing the α
           level to force acceptance of H0).

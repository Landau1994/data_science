5.3.2  The Derived Distributions: Student’s t and Snedecor’s F

The distributions derived in Section 5.3.1 are, in a sense, the first step in a statistical analysis that
assumes normality. In particular, in most practical cases the variance, σ2, is unknown. Thus, to get
any idea of the variability of ¯X (as an estimate of µ), it is necessary to estimate this variance. This
topic was first addressed by W. S. Gosset (who published under the pseudonym of Student) in the
early 1900s. The landmark work of Student resulted in Student’s t distribution or, more simply, the
t distribution.
     If X1,...,Xn are a random sample from a n(µ,σ2), we know that the quantity

is distributed as a n(0,1) random variable. If we knew the value of σ, and we measured ¯X, then
we could use (5.3.3) as a basis for inference about µ, since µ would then be the only unknown
quantity. Most of the time, however, σ is unknown. Student did the obvious thing – he looked at the
distribution of

a quantity that could be used as a basis for inference about µ when σ was unknown.
   The distribution of (5.3.4) is easy to derive, provided that we first notice a few simplifying

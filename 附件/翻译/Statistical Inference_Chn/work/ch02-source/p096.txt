2.26 Let f(x) be a pdf and let a be a number such that, for all ε > 0, f(a+ε) = f(a−ε). Such a pdf is said      to be symmetric about the point a.
       (a) Give three examples of symmetric pdfs.
      (b) Show that if X ∼f(x), symmetric, then the median of X (see Exercise 2.17) is the number a.
       (c) Show that if X ∼f(x), symmetric, and EX exists, then EX = a.
      (d) Show that f(x) = e−x, x ≥0, is not a symmetric pdf.
       (e) Show that for the pdf in part (d), the median is less than the mean.

       (a) Give an example of a unimodal pdf for which the mode is unique.
      (b) Give an example of a unimodal pdf for which the mode is not unique.
       (c) Show that if f(x) is both symmetric (see Exercise 2.26) and unimodal, then the point of symmetry is
         a mode.

   The geometric distribution has an interesting property, known as the “memoryless” property.
For integers s > t, it is the case that

                         P(X > s|X > t) = P(X > s−t);                          (3.2.11)
that is, the geometric distribution “forgets” what has occurred. The probability of getting an addi-
tional s −t failures, having already observed t failures, is the same as the probability of observing
s−t failures at the start of the sequence. In other words, the probability of getting a run of failuresdepends only on the length of the run, not on its position.

   The memoryless property of the geometric distribution describes a very special “lack of aging”
property. It indicates that the geometric distribution is not applicable to modeling lifetimes for which
the probability of failure is expected to increase with time. There are other distributions used to
model various types of aging; see, for example, Barlow and Proschan (1975).

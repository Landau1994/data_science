Hypergeometric Distribution

The hypergeometric distribution has many applications in finite population sampling and is best
understood through the classic example of the urn model.
   Suppose we have a large urn filled with N balls that are identical in every way except that M are
red and N −M are green. We reach in, blindfolded, and select K balls at random (the K balls aretaken all at once, a case of sampling without replacement). What is the probability that exactly x of
the balls are red?
   The total number of samples of size K that can be drawn from the N balls is  NK   , as was
discussed in Section 1.2.3. It is required that x of the balls be red, and this can be accomplished in

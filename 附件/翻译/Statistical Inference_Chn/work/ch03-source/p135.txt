Theorem 3.6.8 (Hwang)   Let g(x) be a function with −∞< Eg(X) < ∞and −∞< g(−1) < ∞.Then
  a.  If X ∼Poisson(λ),
                            E(λg(X)) = E(Xg(X −1)).                            (3.6.5)
  b.  If X ∼negative binomial(r, p),
                                    X
                      E((1−p)g(X)) = E   r +X −1g(X −1)   .                    (3.6.6)
Proof: We will prove part (a), saving part (b) for Exercise 3.50. We have
                                   ∞                x
                      E(λg(X)) =    λg(x)e−λλ                  ∑                                           x=0         x!
                                   ∞           x+1 (x+1)                     =     g(x)e−λλ                  ∑                                           x=0         x!   (x+1)
                                   ∞                 x+1
                     =    (x+1)g(x)e−λλ      .                  ∑                                           x=0         (x+1)!
Now transform the summation index, writing y = x+1. As x goes from 0 to ∞, y goes from 1 to ∞.
Thus,
                               ∞                    y
                 E(λg(X)) = ∑                                     y=1 yg(y−1)e−λλy!
                               ∞                    y
                  =                    (added term is 0)               ∑                                     y=0 yg(y−1)e−λλy!
                  =  E(Xg(X −1)),
since this last sum is a Poisson(λ) expectation.                           2

   Hwang (1982) used his identity in a manner similar to Stein, proving results about multivariate
estimators. The identity has other applications, in particular in moment calculations.

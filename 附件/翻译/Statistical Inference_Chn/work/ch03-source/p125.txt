    In the representation (3.4.1) it is often the case that the dimension of the vector θ is equal to
k, the number of terms in the sum in the exponent. This need not be so, and it is possible for the
dimension of the vector θ to be equal to d < k. Such an exponential family is called a curved
exponential family.
Definition 3.4.7 A curved exponential family is a family of densities of the form (3.4.1) for which
the dimension of the vector θ is equal to d < k. If d = k the family is a full exponential family. (See
also Miscellanea 3.8.3.)

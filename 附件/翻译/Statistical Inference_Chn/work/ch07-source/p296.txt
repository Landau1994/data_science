    In general, we can move in either direction, from the complete-data problem to the incomplete
data problem, or the reverse. If Y = (Y1,Y2 ...,Yn) is the incomplete data, and X = (X1,X2 ...,Xm) is
the augmented data, making (Y,X) the complete data, the densities g(·|θ) of Y and f(·|θ) of (Y,X)have the relationship
                                 g(y|θ) =   f(y,x|θ)dx.                              (7.2.14)
     If we turn these into likelihoods, L(θ|y) = g(y|θ) is the incomplete-data likelihood, and
L(θ|y,x) = f(y,x|θ) is the complete-data likelihood. If L(θ|y) is difficult to work with, it willsometimes be the case that the complete-data likelihood will be easier to work with.

   The EM algorithm allows us to maximize L(θ|y) by only working with L(θ|y,x) and the con-ditional pdf or pmf of X given y and θ, defined by
           =           =       and     = f(y,x|θ) .           (7.2.17)         L(θ|y,x)   f(y,x|θ),  L(θ|y)   g(y|θ),     k(x|θ,y)                                                                g(y|θ)
Rearrangement of the last equation in (7.2.17) gives the identity
                         logL(θ|y) = logL(θ|y,x)−logk(x|θ,y).                     (7.2.18)
As x is missing data, and hence not observed, we replace the right side of (7.2.18) with its expecta-
tion under k(x|θ ′,y), creating the new identity
                 logL(θ|y) = E logL(θ|y,X)|θ ′,y −E logk(X|θ,y)|θ ′,y  .           (7.2.19)

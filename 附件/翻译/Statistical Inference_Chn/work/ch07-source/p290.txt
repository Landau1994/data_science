    In many cases, this simple version of the invariance of MLEs is not useful because many of
the functions we are interested in are not one-to-one. For example, to estimate θ 2, the square of a
normal mean, the mapping θ →θ 2 is not one-to-one. Thus, we need a more general theorem and,in fact, a more general definition of the likelihood function of τ(θ).
     If τ(θ) is not one-to-one, then for a given value η there may be more than one value of θ that
satisfy τ(θ) = η. In such cases, the correspondence between the maximization over η and that over
θ can break down. For example, if ˆθ is the MLE of θ, there may be another value of θ, say θ0, for
which τ( ˆθ) = τ(θ0). We need to avoid such difficulties.
